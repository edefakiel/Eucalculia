📖 Training Guide

What is Eucalculia?

Eucalculia is an evidence-based training tool designed to strengthen number sense through three complementary mechanisms: subitizing (instant quantity recognition), relational evaluation (Posner paradigm applied to number properties), and relational integration (computing and comparing derived numerical relations across hierarchical levels).

The relational integration system (Levels 1 and 2) operationalizes Halford's relational complexity hierarchy in the numerical domain. Level 0 trains binary relational evaluation; Level 1 adds computation on retained values; Level 2 requires comparing across computations — the same cognitive demand that predicts fluid intelligence.

Relational Integration Levels

Level 0 — Relational Evaluation Posner

Two quantities flash side by side, then disappear. A relational question appears: "Same parity?", "Sum > 10?", etc. You answer YES or NO. You must encode both quantities as abstract numerical representations because the question is hidden during presentation.

Level 1 — Cross-pair numeric questions Integration

After two Level 0 trials you are asked a numeric question that combines both pairs — e.g. "Sum of all four values?" or "Difference of the two maximums?". There is no hint: both pairs must be retained in working memory as abstract numbers. Enter the answer on the numpad.

This is the critical step: Level 0 only requires evaluating a relation; Level 1 requires retaining values and computing new ones — the transition from relational evaluation to relational integration.

Level 2 — Meta² conclusion selection Integration

After two Level 1 trials, Meta² no longer asks for a simple YES/NO response. It presents four compact semantic conclusions about the two computed answers and the L1 rules that produced them. Exactly one conclusion is fully true.

The distractors are controlled transformations: one may reverse the comparison, another may flip a metric property such as parity or gap size, and another may alter operation provenance. You must construct the relation, not merely recognize a surface wording.

Level 3 — Meta³-questions Integration

After two Level 2 trials, Meta³ now presents four compact summaries of the two complete Meta² episodes. Exactly one synthesis is correct. The episode includes the correct L2 conclusion, relation family, answer-gap, threshold/source status, operation provenance and internal relational-frame tags.

Ground truth is based on the correct previous Meta² episodes, not your selected options. If you got an L2 wrong, the REVIEW feedback gives the correct conclusion; use it to update the retained episode before L3 arrives. This keeps Meta³ focused on synthesis between conclusions rather than on preserving an earlier mistake.

Block Structure (with Meta-levels)

Trials follow nested cycles: L0 → L0 → L1 repeats twice to set up an L2, and two L2s set up an L3. At Meta³ enabled, the full cycle is 15 trials. L1 spans the two preceding L0 pairs, L2 spans the two preceding L1 answers, and L3 spans the two preceding L2 episodes. The hierarchy is now: relation → numeric construction → semantic conclusion → synthesis between conclusions.

Level 0 Question Tiers

v5.9.4 uses RFT-style isomorphic frames as active training metadata, not as a separate evaluation layer. Coordination (same number / equal value / same quantity), opposition (not the same value / different parity), comparison (left greater than right / right smaller than left), inversion (left > right / right < left) and role-binding (larger value / smaller value / value closer to 10) all remain in the live training pool, while recent errors softly increase the frequency of the relevant frame families.

Tier 1 — Direct comparison (all levels)
Same number? — Do both displays represent the same quantity? This ignores visual format; format is asked separately.
Left > Right? — Is the left quantity larger?
Differ by 1? — Are they consecutive numbers?
Same representation? — Are both shown in the same visual format?
Tier 2 — Single property (level 2+)
Same parity? — Both even or both odd?
Both > 5? — Are both quantities greater than five?
Both ≤ 4? — Are both in the small-number range?
Sum > 10? — Does their sum exceed ten?
Sum = 10? — Do they add up to exactly ten?
Tier 3 — Compound reasoning (level 3+)
Difference > 3? — Is the gap more than 3?
Sum is even? — Is their sum an even number?
Left is double? — Is left exactly twice right?
Both prime? — Are both prime numbers?
Product > 20? — Does their product exceed 20?
Difference is even? — Is the gap even?
Both multiples of 3? — Are both divisible by three?
Tier 4 — Multi-step reasoning (level 5+)
One divides the other? — Is one a factor of the other?
Sum is prime? — Is their sum a prime number?
Exactly one even? — Is precisely one even?
Gap > half the larger? — Difference exceeds half the max?
Both square? — Are both perfect squares?
Left ≥ 3× Right? — Is left at least triple right?
Role-binding extensions (v5.5)
Larger value is even / square / closer to 10? — First identify the larger value, then test a property of that role.
Smaller value is odd / divides larger? — First identify the smaller value, then test parity or factor structure.
Gap is 2 / prime / equal to the smaller value? — Treat the difference itself as the object of reasoning.
Left closer to 5 / value closer to 10 is even? — Bind numerical magnitude to a reference-point relation.
Larger = 2×smaller + 1? — Test a composed multiplicative-plus-offset relation.

Level 1 Operations

Operational-relational derivations on retained pairs
Global summaries — Total, largest, smallest, range, middle values and extreme values across all four quantities
Within-pair roles — Larger/smaller value of each pair, cross-role combinations such as smaller of pair 1 plus larger of pair 2
Gap relations — Add, compare or select the internal differences of the two pairs
Positional relations — Left-side total, right-side total, and gaps between first or second displayed values
Base-10 anchors — Distance of selected derived values from 10, especially minima/maxima combinations
Conditional selections — Enter a value, gap or total from the pair selected by a relation such as higher total, lower total, closer pair or wider-gap pair
Property counts — Count even, prime, distinct, greater-than-five, or same-parity patterns across the four retained values

Level 1 functions as a reconfiguration engine: the same four retained values can be reorganized by magnitude, role, position, gap, base-10 distance, property count or conditional pair selection. v5.9.3 keeps all natural, compact mathematical, procedural and role-based formulations active, but softly increases the frequency of frames that have recently produced errors. Directional difference operations still choose wording that yields a non-negative answer; conditional tie cases are stated in the prompt.

Level 2 Semantic Conclusion Selection

A. Answer relations
Magnitude and equality — which answer was larger, or whether both were equal.
Parity and combined totals — same/different parity, even/odd combined total.
Calibrated thresholds — answer gap ≤ N, combined total > N, or both answers > N, with N adapted to the exact L1 operation pair.
B. Derived metric relations
Gap properties — even/odd gap, prime/non-prime gap, larger/smaller gap logic.
Multiplicative relations — larger at least double, strict factor relation.
Base-10 relations — equal distance from 10, or which answer is closer to 10.
C. Operation-source relations
Operation group — whether the two L1 rules came from the same broad family.
Gap and position dependency — whether both rules matched in their use of internal gaps or left/right position.
Source-to-answer binding — whether the gap-based or position-based L1 rule produced the larger answer.

L2 now presents four semantic conclusions. The correct option is either a compact two-clause conclusion or a bound source→answer conclusion; distractors flip one clause, the source/result bind, a metric property, a threshold relation, or operation provenance. This preserves the closed-response efficiency of the game while making the response more constructive than YES/NO.

Level 3 Meta³ synthesis

Level 3 now asks for a four-option summary of two previous Meta² episodes. It does not ask for a YES/NO truth-state; it asks which synthesis correctly binds episode structure to numerical outcomes.

A. Episode synthesis
Winner/gap summaries — same or switched winner side, same or different answer-gap parity, or which episode had the larger answer-gap.
Family/gap binds — whether the answer, metric, threshold or source episode carried the larger answer-gap.
B. Frame synthesis
Frame use — whether one or both episodes used reversal, role binding, source relation, metric relation or threshold relation.
Frame→episode binds — e.g. whether the episode using reversal or threshold had the larger or smaller answer-gap.

L3 remains numerical: all summaries are derived from L1 answers, L2 conclusions and their operation-source metadata. The four options are near-misses, with distractors built by flipping the relevant bind or relational frame.

Adaptive System

Subitizing mode: each number has its own exposure time (ET). Correct fast responses shorten ET; errors increase it. Less-mastered numbers appear more frequently. Level-up when all values at the current range are mastered.

Relational mode: a protected global ET applies to L0 stimuli — individual values are not the unit of learning, relations are. Unlike subitizing, relational exposure has a higher floor, slower acceleration, and temporary novelty floors for newly introduced categories. Level-up requires a rolling window of L0 trials at the current level with ≥85% accuracy and average correct RT ≤1200 ms (window size scales with level difficulty).

Level 0 categories are filtered by whether the current value range supports them (e.g. "Sum > 10?" only appears once the range is large enough). Each L0 trial is an independent Bernoulli(0.5) over YES/NO: the generator draws a target outcome first, then builds a pair that satisfies it — so YES and NO are structurally balanced, not statistically hoped-for.

L0 categories are first selected by family — direct comparison, joint property, or role/property relation — with the role/property family becoming more prominent at higher levels. Within the selected family, categories are weighted by per-category accuracy and RT. L1 operations are weighted by accuracy. L0, L1, L2 and L3 now carry internal relational-frame tags — coordination, opposition, comparison, reversal, role-binding, conditional, source, metric and threshold — so recent errors can softly increase the frequency of weaker verbal-relational frames. L2 converts selected relations into four-option semantic conclusion tasks. L3 converts two L2 episodes into four-option semantic synthesis tasks.
